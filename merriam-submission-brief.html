2 · Artist Statement v2 · 2,957 chars

Anchored on Mission Hills + the trail's "urban Zen zone" framing from the Master Plan, then Mark's archaeological-dig-of-childhood logic, then team + approach. Paste into CaFÉ as-is.

My grandparents settled in Mission Hills forty years ago, and I lived there too — six miles from this site. I have walked the Turkey Creek Streamway Trail and watched Merriam's public art evolve from Still Time through Bask to the Milagros Collective work that just opened at the Community Center. The Master Plan and Kansas City Hiker both call this trail an urban Zen zone. They're right — it is one of those rare municipal landscapes that lets you exhale.

That exhale is exactly where Misfit Toys' work meets people. Our founder Mark Deem has been chasing one question for close to a decade: what would it look like to stumble across an archaeological dig of your childhood? The answer, we've learned, has to be massive. The scale isn't spectacle — it's the mechanism. When a grown adult walks up to a jax the size of a Mini Cooper, or alphabet blocks taller than they are, something involuntary happens. They're catapulted out of transit brain into a childlike state of discovery. A trail walker, a kid in a wheelchair, a seventy-year-old with a coffee — all understand what a block is, what a jax is, what it felt like to be small in the world. We don't ask them to remember. We put them back there.

Since 2013, Misfit Toys LLC has produced large-scale interactive sculpture seen by more than eight million visitors between Golden Gate Park and the Black Rock Desert. We partner with SF Recreation & Parks, the Presidio Trust and National Park Service, the Burning Man Project (Honoraria 2025; Man Crew 2013–present), Illuminate the Arts, Building 180, and the Sijbrandij Foundation. Our Golden Mile installations contributed to a documented 36% increase in park visits. We know permits, design review, ADA accessibility, and the cadence of municipal partnership.

Our team is purpose-built for this site. Mark Deem brings biomedical-engineering rigor to large-scale steel. Joe Howard, M.L.A., is a licensed California landscape architect who led the design conversion of the Presidio of San Francisco from Army base to public trust. Carrie Barnes runs the 75-person Man Pavilion construction crew at Burning Man. Anthony Vincent, shop steward at Reno Generator, moves monumental steel from CAD to ground on deadline. Bobby Reagan is our regional crew member, based an hour north in St. Joseph, Missouri. Up to thirty rotating volunteers support the core.

We design sculpture that invites a wheelchair user, a five-year-old, and a seventy-year-old to use the same piece in three different ways. We build for eastern Kansas weather — hot-dip galvanized steel, weather-resistant finishes, footings engineered for installation by Merriam Public Works crews. And we listen first: to the creek, to the butterfly garden, to the community that already walks this corridor every day.

The Streamway Trail's Zen zone deserves a sculpture that meets it on its own terms — present, generous, unforgettable. We would be honored to be selected as semi-finalists.
