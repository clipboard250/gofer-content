How to Get Hired
Don't email. Don't DM. Walk in. Small shop owners respond to a real face and real enthusiasm way more than an application they'll never open. Here's the full playbook.
```
3–4
shops a day, in person. Most will say no or not right now. That's fine. One yes and you're in.
01

Walk in—don't call, don't email

A voicemail from a 16-year-old gets ignored. A 16-year-old standing in front of them, clearly serious? That's different. Hit them on a Tuesday or Wednesday morning when it's slow. Wear closed-toe shoes—look like you could start today.

02

Say this

"Hey, my name's Abraham. I'm 16, I'm homeschooled so I can work full-time, and I'm serious about learning this trade. Is there any way I could contribute here and earn something while I learn?"

That's it. Short, direct, no begging.

03

Bring a one-page resume

Even a short one. Most 16-year-olds don't do this. You will. Your name, phone number, "available full-time—homeschooled," anything you've fixed or built (even informal stuff), and one person who can vouch for you. That's enough.

04

Know something about their work

Before you walk into a moto shop, look up one bike model they probably work on. Before a classic car shop, know one build you've seen online. You don't need to be an expert—you just need to show you're actually interested, not just looking for any job.

05

If they say "insurance"—don't just leave

Some shop owners genuinely want to help but their policy won't allow under-18s on the floor. If you hear that, say: "I completely understand. Is there anything outside the service area—parts, phones, cleanup—I could help with while you figure that out?" Give them a way to still say yes.

06

Follow up exactly one week later

Most people don't. Showing back up when you said you would—just to check in, no pressure—tells a shop owner more about your character than anything you said the first time. That's what gets people hired.

What goes on your resume
  • Name, phone, "Available full-time · Homeschooled"—say it clearly up front
  • Anything you've fixed or built, even if nobody paid you for it. Helped a neighbor? Fixed your own car? That counts.
  • Volunteer hours (St. Pete Bike Co-op, church, school, anything hands-on with tools)
  • One sentence about yourself: "16-year-old in St. Pete who wants to learn automotive and mechanics hands-on—available full-time and serious about it."
  • One reference. A teacher, a neighbor, anyone who can say "he shows up and he works hard."
  • Keep it one page. Clean. No fluff. If it fits on half a page, that's fine too.
```
